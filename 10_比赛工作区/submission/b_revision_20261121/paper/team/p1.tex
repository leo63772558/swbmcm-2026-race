To characterize every ground position from which a mobile vehicle can legally station itself and observe hovering alert T1—located at $T_1=(50+40\cos 30^\circ,\; 50+40\sin 30^\circ,\; 45)\approx(84.641016,\; 70.000000,\; 45)$ m—we construct the vehicle-center feasible set $\mathcal{F}(T_1)$ as the exact intersection of three distinct geometric constraints: planar body safety, gimbal elevation bounds, and three-dimensional building line-of-sight clearance.

\subsection{Exact Euclidean Rounded Minkowski Safety Space and Closure Separation}
Because each vehicle occupies a rigid horizontal disk $D(0,r_v)$ of radius $r_v=1.5$ m, a candidate station $p=(x,y)$ keeps the vehicle body strictly inside the $[0,100]^2$ field if and only if $p\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{in}}=[1.5,\,98.5]^2$. To prevent chassis contact with any rectangular building footprint $B_k=[x_{k,\min},x_{k,\max}]\times[y_{k,\min},y_{k,\max}]$ ($k=1,\dots,5$), we compute the exact Euclidean Minkowski sum \cite{lozano1983}
\[
B_k^{\oplus} = B_k \oplus D(0,r_v) = \bigl\{p\in\mathbb{R}^2 : \operatorname{dist}_2(p,B_k) < r_v\bigr\},
\]
whose boundary $\partial B_k^{\oplus}$ consists of four straight wall offsets and four quarter-circular fillets of radius $r_v=1.5$ m centered at the building corners. Because chassis tangency is permitted, the body-safe configuration space is the closed set
\[
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{free}} = [1.5,\,98.5]^2 \setminus \bigcup_{k=1}^{5} \operatorname{int}\!\bigl(B_k \oplus D(0,r_v)\bigr).
\]
In contrast, optical visibility obeys an open-interior occlusion test: for camera mount height $h_c=2.0$ m, the visibility set $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{LOS}}(T_1)$ comprises all points $p=(x,y)$ such that the closed 3D ray segment $\overline{(x,y,h_c)\,T_1}$ has empty intersection with every closed building prism $B_k\times[0,H_k]$. Evaluating ray-box intersections with exact floating-point slab predicates \cite{shewchuk1997} separates closed-disk chassis tangency from closed-prism optical occlusion, yielding the complete observable set
\[
\mathcal{F}(T_1) = \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{free}} \cap \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{LOS}}(T_1) \cap \bigl\{p\in\mathbb{R}^2 : r(p) \ge r_{\min}(\beta_{\max})\bigr\},
\]
where $r(p)=\|p-T_1'\|_2$ is the horizontal range from $p$ to the ground projection $T_1'$ of T1. Integrating this region across progressively refined grids (Table~\ref{tab:p1} and Figure~\ref{fig:p1}a) converges cleanly to an observable area of $\BGridArea{}$ m\textsuperscript{2} at 0.1 m resolution (1,000,000 grid cells), independently corroborated by 250,000 fixed-seed Monte Carlo samples ($5613.36$ m\textsuperscript{2}, 95\% confidence interval $[5593.90,\,5632.80]$ m\textsuperscript{2}).

\subsection{Optimal Inner Arc, Pitch Sensitivity, and Readiness-First Stationing}
Since observation quality $Q(r)=1/r$ decreases strictly with horizontal range $r$ and the elevation angle $\beta(r)=\arctan((H-h_c)/r)$ is capped at $\beta_{\max}=\BMaxPitch{}^\circ$ (while the lower bound $-10^\circ$ is inactive for $H=45\text{ m}>h_c=2\text{ m}$), quality attains its theoretical ceiling along the concentric inner circle of radius $r_{\min}(\beta_{\max})=(45-2)/\tan 80^\circ=\BMinRange{}$ m, giving $Q_{\max}=\BQuality{}$ m\textsuperscript{$-1$}. Intersecting this circle with $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{free}}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{LOS}}(T_1)$ reveals a single contiguous optimal arc around T1 spanning unwrapped azimuths from $\BArcStart{}^\circ$ to $\BArcEnd{}^\circ$ with exact arc length $\BArcLength{}$ m (Figure~\ref{fig:p1}b). Both boundary endpoints lie strictly outside the closed building shadows and satisfy the closed-disk body tangency condition against building $B_2$'s rounded safety contour; accordingly, both endpoints are verified feasible and included on the closed optimal arc $[\BArcStart{}^\circ,\,\BArcEnd{}^\circ]$, directly consolidating our closed-tangency versus closed-occlusion boundary specification without relying on external addenda.

Differentiating the maximum quality $Q_{\max}(\beta_{\max})=\tan\beta_{\max}/(H-h_c)$ yields the first-order analytical sensitivity given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p1-quality}: $\mathrm{d}Q_{\max}/\mathrm{d}\beta_{\max}=\sec^2\beta_{\max}/(H-h_c)$. Because $\beta_{\max}=80^\circ$ sits close to the $\tan\beta$ asymptote where $\sec^2 80^\circ\approx 33.16$, even a $1^\circ$ reduction in gimbal elevation ceiling ($80^\circ\!\to\!79^\circ$) expands $r_{\min}$ from $\BMinRange{}$ m to $8.358353$ m and triggers a disproportionate $\BPitchLoss{}\%$ drop in peak observation quality (and a $69.46\%$ loss at $60^\circ$, Figure~\ref{fig:p1}d). This analytical curvature directly foreshadows why $\beta_{\max}$ emerges as the single strongest parameter in our global sensitivity audit.

Every point on the optimal arc achieves identical quality $Q_{\max}=\BQuality{}$ m\textsuperscript{$-1$}, making the tie-breaking rule decisive. Under our system-wide \emph{readiness-first} criterion (minimizing total time to complete both ground transit and gimbal slewing), vehicle V4 traveling from $(88,68)$ m to $(\BStationX{},\,\BStationY{})$ m at arc azimuth $\BReadyAngle{}^\circ$ requires $\BArrivalTime{}$ s of driving plus $2.666667$ s of simultaneous pan/tilt slewing for a total readiness time of $\BReadyTime{}$ s (certified to lie within an interval width of $7.74\times 10^{-5}$ s). For transparency against an alternative travel-only interpretation, Table~\ref{tab:p1} also reports the pure arrival-first witness on the same arc (V4 at $329.23^\circ$, driving $\BAltArrival{}$ s but requiring $4.974333$ s of slewing for $6.198580$ s readiness—over $1.13$ s slower to begin observation).

Finally, replacing conservative axis-aligned bounding-box (AABB) inflation with exact Euclidean rounded corners yields tangible geometric gains across the urban canyon.
