Problem 2(2) scales the single-target assignment into a tightly coupled multi-vehicle scheduling problem: five vehicles ($V_1$--$V_5$) must jointly observe three simultaneous alerts (T1, T2, and T3) released at $t=0$ s, where each alert requires two distinct vehicles stationed at non-collinear unobstructed positions for a synchronized $\tau_{\mathrm{obs}}=5.0$ s dwell. Because six observation slots must be served by five vehicles, at least one vehicle must execute two sequential assignments separated by a mandatory gimbal reset and inter-station transit \cite{psaraftis1980,savelsbergh1995}.

\subsection{Coupled Six-Slot Mathematical Program and Phase Clocks}
Let $\mathcal{V}=\{1,\dots,5\}$ denote the fleet and $\mathcal{A}=\{T_1,T_2,T_3\}$ the alert set, with each alert $a\in\mathcal{A}$ associated with its rounded-clearance feasible station pool $\mathcal{S}(a)$ (585 total discrete stations across T1--T3). A feasible dispatch plan assigns to every alert $a$ an unordered pair of distinct vehicles $\{i_1(a), i_2(a)\}\subset\mathcal{V}$ and distinct stations $(s_{i_1},s_{i_2})\in\mathcal{S}(a)^2$ satisfying the non-collinearity condition $0^\circ < \gamma(s_{i_1},s_{i_2};a) < 180^\circ$. For a vehicle's first slot (departure clock $t_i^{\mathrm{dep}}=0$), its arrival and gimbal readiness clocks are
\[
t_i^{\mathrm{arr}} = t_i^{\mathrm{dep}} + \frac{d_{\mathrm{rnd}}(p_i^{\mathrm{init}}, s_i)}{v}, \qquad
t_i^{\mathrm{slew}} = t_i^{\mathrm{arr}} + \max\!\left(\frac{|\Delta\alpha(s_i,a)|}{\omega_{\mathrm{pan}}},\,\frac{|\Delta\beta(s_i,a)|}{\omega_{\mathrm{tilt}}}\right).
\]
Paired observation of alert $a$ commences only when both assigned partners finish slewing, $t_a^{\mathrm{start}}=\max(t_{i_1}^{\mathrm{slew}},t_{i_2}^{\mathrm{slew}})$ as stated in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p2-dispatch}, and finishes at $t_a^{\mathrm{end}}=t_a^{\mathrm{start}}+\tau_{\mathrm{obs}}$. If vehicle $i$ is reused on a second alert $a_2$ after completing $a_1$, it must first return its gimbal to $(0^\circ,0^\circ)$ before departing at release time $t_i^{\mathrm{rel}} = t_{a_1}^{\mathrm{end}} + \tau_{\mathrm{rst}}(s_{i,a_1})$, where $\tau_{\mathrm{rst}}$ equals its initial slew duration. The objective is to minimize the latest paired observation start time $t_{\mathrm{last}}=\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}} t_a^{\mathrm{start}}$.

\subsection{Shortlist-Induced Ranking Reversal ($k=3$ vs.\ $k=8$) and Exact B\&B Certification}
A naive comparison across heuristics can easily conflate search quality with candidate-pool filtering. To enforce a strictly matched experimental design, both our Beam Search dispatcher and the greedy Nearest baseline draw from identical arrival-sorted shortlists $\mathcal{S}_k(i,a)$ under the same rounded-clearance routing function (Algorithm~\ref{alg:p2-dispatch}). Table~\ref{tab:p22}, Figure~\ref{fig:p2paths}, and Figure~\ref{fig:p22} reveal a striking \emph{ranking reversal} as shortlist depth $k_{\mathrm{cand}}$ expands from $3$ to $8$.

At $k_{\mathrm{cand}}=3$ (beam width $w=20$), Beam Search delivers $t_{\mathrm{last}}=\BBeamThree{}$ s—actually $1.345$ s \emph{slower} than the Nearest baseline ($\BNearestThree{}$ s). Inspecting the per-vehicle phase ledger (Figure~\ref{fig:p22}c--d) exposes the exact structural cause: pruning to three stations per vehicle excludes two pivotal synchronization stations—V1's rank-8 station at $(4,20)$ m for T2 and V2's rank-5 station at $(84,4)$ m for T3. Deprived of those stations, Beam Search assigns reused vehicle V3 to observe T3 first and T1 second ($\mathrm{T3}\!\to\!\mathrm{T1}$), forcing V3 to wait $1.063$ s for partner V4 on T3 before resetting at $12.085$ s and finally opening T1 at $\BBeamThree{}$ s.

Expanding the shared shortlist to $k_{\mathrm{cand}}=8$ immediately restores those two higher-rank stations (Figure~\ref{fig:p2paths}c) and flips the critical reuse chain on V3 to $\mathrm{T1}\!\to\!\mathrm{T3}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:p2paths}a). Under this unlocked topology, V3 drives $2.108$ s to $(12,92)$ m, slews $0.984$ s, and initiates paired observation of T1 with V5 at $3.093$ s with \emph{zero partner wait}; after dwelling 5.0 s and resetting in $0.984$ s, V3 remains stationary at $(12,92)$ m (zero inter-alert transit!), slews $1.957$ s directly onto T3, and starts paired observation with V2 at exactly $t_{\mathrm{last}}=\BBeamEight{}$ s, while V1 and V4 complete T2 in parallel at $8.890$ s. Meanwhile, the Nearest baseline ignores global reuse coupling and remains frozen at $\BNearestEight{}$ s (reusing V5 on $\mathrm{T1}\!\to\!\mathrm{T3}$), giving Beam Search a verified $0.690$ s lead.

To test whether beam pruning ($w=20$) or the $k=8$ shortlist misses any superior discrete schedule, we implemented an independent, zero-truncation exact Branch-and-Bound solver (Algorithm~\ref{alg:p2-dispatch}, lines 6--14) that exhaustively traverses all $5 \times 6 \times \binom{4}{2} \times 2 = 360$ vehicle-to-alert assignment topologies (pruning 67 dominated branches via tight solo-pair and partner-readiness lower bounds). As documented in Table~\ref{tab:p22}, exact Branch-and-Bound returns $t_{\mathrm{last}}^*=11.034062$ s on the $k=8$ pool \emph{and} identically $11.034062$ s when the shortlist cap is removed completely across the entire 585-station discrete pool—certifying a $0.000000$ s optimality gap. In keeping with rigorous scientific reporting, we emphasize that this zero-gap certificate applies strictly to the 585-station discrete candidate pool and rounded-clearance waypoint graph rather than an unconstrained continuum over $[1.5,98.5]^2$.
