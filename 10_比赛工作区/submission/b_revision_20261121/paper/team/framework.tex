A central methodological pitfall in multi-part modeling benchmarks is treating each subproblem as an isolated mathematical exercise with incompatible geometries, inconsistent tie-breaking rules, or mismatched schedules. To prevent cross-stage drift, we engineer a tightly coupled, four-stage interdisciplinary pipeline in which verified data structures flow strictly downstream from computational geometry through combinatorial optimization and stochastic analysis to discrete-event simulation \cite{vantendeloo2018}. Figure~\ref{fig:framework} illustrates the end-to-end architecture and verified numeric handoffs linking Problems 1 through 4.

At the foundation lies a single \textbf{Shared Geometry Engine} encoding the $100\text{ m}\times 100\text{ m}$ urban field, five closed building prisms, the inset boundary $[1.5,98.5]^2$, exact Euclidean rounded Minkowski clearance, elevation bounds, and three-dimensional ray-box occlusion predicates. \textbf{Problem 1} directly queries this engine to delineate the ground observable set $\mathcal{F}(T_1)$, characterize its boundary arcs, evaluate inverse-range quality $Q=1/r$, and establish the global readiness-first tie-breaking convention used uniformly across all downstream modules.

Moving to \textbf{Problem 2}, the exact same feasible station pools and rounded-clearance road network feed two coupled solvers. In Problem 2(1), feasible station pairs within the 30 s arrival radius of alert T2 are evaluated through the linearized bearing-intersection Jacobian to minimize local horizontal root-mean-square (RMS) error. In Problem 2(2), candidate stations for simultaneous alerts T1, T2, and T3 are passed into our shared-shortlist cooperative dispatcher—and independently audited by a zero-truncation exact Branch-and-Bound solver over all 360 vehicle-alert topologies—producing the nominal $k=8$ schedule with latest paired start $t_{\mathrm{last}}=\BBeamEight{}$ s. Critically, this nominal schedule is cryptographically frozen (plan digest verified before every downstream run) so that all subsequent resilience evaluations and visual replays operate on the exact same baseline trajectory set.

In parallel, \textbf{Problem 3} bridges open-loop navigation drift and operational routing by bounding how far any vehicle may travel before isotropic odometry error $\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}(s)=a+bs$ and gimbal imperfections degrade horizontal pointing coverage below 95\%. The resulting unique admissible distance threshold $s_{\max}=\BIntegratedCrossing{}$ m directly audits the feasibility of our P2 dispatch trajectories: in the nominal $k=8$ schedule, the longest single-vehicle excursion is only $12.53$ m—less than one-fifth of $s_{\max}$—confirming ample pointing-reliability margin. Finally, \textbf{Problem 4} initializes a discrete-event simulator \cite{vantendeloo2018} from the frozen nominal schedule and replays all \BCaseCount{} disturbance scenarios (hardware faults, pop-up alerts, and communication blackouts) under both Keep Plan and Reschedule policies, scoring every scenario lexicographically via $(\eta,-t_{\mathrm{last}})$. The verified execution intervals then drive our global sensitivity analysis, multi-objective Pareto comparison, and offline 3D digital twin validation.
