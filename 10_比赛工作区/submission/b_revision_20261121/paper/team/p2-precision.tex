Problem 2(1) asks for a pair of mobile observation vehicles that can depart from their initial positions, reach unobstructed stations around hovering alert T2—located at $T_2=(50+45\cos 150^\circ,\; 50+45\sin 150^\circ,\; 35)\approx(11.028857,\; 72.500000,\; 35)$ m—within a 30 s travel-time budget, and minimize the horizontal triangulation error under independent bearing noise of standard deviation $\sigma_\theta=1^\circ=\pi/180$ rad. Because target altitude $H_{T2}=35$ m is known, horizontal localization reduces to intersecting two noisy azimuth rays in $\mathbb{R}^2$ \cite{bishop2010}.

\subsection{First-Order Azimuth Jacobian and Covariance Derivation}
Let two vehicles occupy distinct feasible stations $S_1=(x_1,y_1)$ and $S_2=(x_2,y_2)$ in $\mathcal{F}(T_2)$ at horizontal ranges $r_i=\sqrt{(x-x_i)^2+(y-y_i)^2}$ and true bearings $\alpha_i=\operatorname{atan2}(y-y_i,\,x-x_i)$ toward the target ground projection $T_2'=(x,y)$. The noisy bearing measurement vector is $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}(x,y)+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$ with $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma_\theta^2 I_2)$. Differentiating $\alpha_i(x,y)$ with respect to the horizontal target coordinates yields
\[
\frac{\partial \alpha_i}{\partial x} = -\frac{y - y_i}{r_i^2} = -\frac{\sin\alpha_i}{r_i}, \qquad
\frac{\partial \alpha_i}{\partial y} = \frac{x - x_i}{r_i^2} = \frac{\cos\alpha_i}{r_i},
\]
which assemble directly into the $2\times 2$ measurement Jacobian $J$ stated in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p2-rms}. Applying first-order Gauss--Newton propagation around the true target location produces the local error covariance matrix $\Sigma_p = \sigma_\theta^2 (J^\top J)^{-1}$. Because $|\det(J)| = |\sin(\alpha_2-\alpha_1)|/(r_1 r_2) = \sin\gamma/(r_1 r_2)$, where $\gamma=|\operatorname{wrap}_{[-\pi,\pi)}(\alpha_2-\alpha_1)|\in(0,\pi)$ is the acute or obtuse crossing angle between the two lines of sight, inverting the $2\times 2$ Fisher information matrix $J^\top J$ yields the closed-form trace
\[
\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_p) = \sigma_\theta^2 \frac{\|J\|_F^2}{(\det J)^2} = \sigma_\theta^2 \frac{r_1^{-2} + r_2^{-2}}{\sin^2\gamma / (r_1^2 r_2^2)} = \frac{\sigma_\theta^2 (r_1^2 + r_2^2)}{\sin^2\gamma},
\]
whose square root yields the closed-form local first-order horizontal RMS error $\mathrm{RMS}_{\mathrm{local}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p2-rms}. This expression exposes two competing geometric levers: shrinking the stand-off ranges $r_1,r_2$ toward the elevation-limited inner circle $r_{\min}(T_2)=(35-2)/\tan 80^\circ=5.818790$ m contracts the numerator, while positioning the two vehicles in orthogonal intersection ($\gamma=90^\circ$, $|\sin\gamma|=1$) maximizes the denominator. We formalize this global optimality certificate in Proposition~\ref{prop:p21-lb}.

\begin{keybox}[top=4pt,bottom=4pt]
\small\setlength{\abovedisplayskip}{4pt}\setlength{\belowdisplayskip}{4pt}
\begin{proposition}[Analytical Lower Bound and Exact Attainment for Dual-Station Localization]
\label{prop:p21-lb}
For any pair of distinct feasible stations $S_1,S_2\in\mathcal{F}(T_2)$ obeying the gimbal elevation bound $\beta(r_i)\le\beta_{\max}=80^\circ$ ($i=1,2$) and independent bearing noise $\sigma_\theta>0$, the local first-order horizontal RMS error satisfies the universal lower bound
\[
\mathrm{RMS}_{\mathrm{local}}(S_1,S_2) = \frac{\sigma_\theta\sqrt{r_1^2+r_2^2}}{|\sin\gamma|} \ge \sqrt{2}\,\sigma_\theta\,r_{\min}(T_2),
\qquad r_{\min}(T_2)=\frac{H_{T2}-h_c}{\tan\beta_{\max}}=5.818790\text{ m},
\]
with equality if and only if $r_1=r_2=r_{\min}(T_2)$ and the lines of sight intersect orthogonally ($\gamma=90^\circ$).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every feasible station $S_i\in\mathcal{F}(T_2)$ must satisfy $\arctan((H_{T2}-h_c)/r_i)\le\beta_{\max}$, which is equivalent to $r_i\ge r_{\min}(T_2)>0$; hence $\sqrt{r_1^2+r_2^2}\ge\sqrt{2}\,r_{\min}(T_2)$. Simultaneously, $0<|\sin\gamma|\le 1$ for every non-collinear crossing angle $\gamma\in(0,\pi)$, with equality uniquely at $\gamma=\pi/2$. Dividing the two inequalities yields $\mathrm{RMS}_{\mathrm{local}}\ge\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_\theta r_{\min}(T_2)=\sqrt{2}(\pi/180)(33/\tan 80^\circ)=0.143623358\text{ m}$. Because stations $S_1=(6.914351,68.385494)$ m and $S_2=(6.914351,76.614506)$ m lie strictly inside $[1.5,98.5]^2$ with clear 3D line of sight, rounded Minkowski obstacle clearance, exact ranges $r_1=r_2=r_{\min}(T_2)$, orthogonal crossing $\gamma=90^\circ$, and rounded-path arrival times $6.459\text{ s},15.365\text{ s}\le 30\text{ s}$ for V3 and V5, this unconstrained analytical lower bound is attained exactly on the constrained feasible set.
\end{proof}
\end{keybox}

\subsection{30 s Reachability Routing, Optimal Geometry, and Degeneracy Boundary}
To verify the 30 s travel-time ceiling without violating rounded obstacle safety, we construct a visibility graph over the inset domain $[1.5,98.5]^2$ augmented with safely offset building-corner waypoints and solve shortest paths via Dijkstra's algorithm \cite{dijkstra1959,hart1968} on edges verified for continuous Euclidean rounded clearance. Optimizing Eq.~\eqref{eq:p2-rms} over all feasible vehicle pairs and reachable stations in $\mathcal{F}(T_2)$ identifies vehicles \textbf{V3} and \textbf{V5} stationed symmetrically along the western flank of T2's optimal elevation ring at $S_1=(6.914351,\,68.385494)$ m and $S_2=(6.914351,\,76.614506)$ m (Table~\ref{tab:p21}). Both stations attain the minimum elevation-permitted horizontal range $r_1=r_2=5.818790$ m while forming an exact orthogonal crossing angle $\gamma=\BCrossing{}^\circ$ across baseline length $B=\BBaseLength{}$ m.

Under rounded-clearance routing, V3 reaches $S_1$ in $\BTravelFirst{}$ s and V5 reaches $S_2$ in $\BTravelSecond{}$ s—both comfortably inside the 30 s budget with more than 14 s of reserve margin. At this configuration, the analytical covariance matrix is perfectly isotropic ($\Sigma_{xx}=\Sigma_{yy}=0.010314$ m\textsuperscript{2}, $\Sigma_{xy}=0$), producing a circular 95\% error ellipse (Figure~\ref{fig:p21}b) and an optimal local horizontal RMS error of $\BFirstRMS{}$ m. Independent central finite-difference perturbation of the nonlinear triangulation equations reproduces the exact same value ($0.143623358$ m, relative discrepancy $<10^{-10}$), validating the Jacobian derivation.

As $\gamma\to0^\circ$, the two bearings point in almost the same direction. As $\gamma\to180^\circ$, opposed bearings approach the same line and give the same rank deficiency, even though the baseline approaches $r_1+r_2$. In both limits $|\det J|\to0$, so the local RMS grows without bound at fixed positive ranges (Figure~\ref{fig:p21}a). Reject collinear pairs and screen near-collinear pairs symmetrically, for example by keeping $\gamma\in[30^\circ,150^\circ]$. The feasible $90^\circ$ pair in \texttt{P2\_precision.csv} already attains Proposition~\ref{prop:p21-lb}'s lower bound, so this screen preserves the nominal optimum.

At $r_i=r_{\min}$ the pitch constraint is active, but the bearing Jacobian remains nonsingular for interior crossing angles. The nominal stations have zero pitch margin, so an inward range perturbation can violate observability even while the nominal localization error stays finite. A LOS boundary needs separate treatment because contact with a building prism blocks observation. Tightening the allowable elevation to $79^\circ$ moves the T2 inner radius to $\BPrecisionTightRange{}$ m. Moving both stations radially outward preserves the orthogonal intersection. Both stations remain visible and reachable within 30 s, giving an attained minimum RMS of $\BPrecisionTightRMS{}$ m, a $\BPrecisionTightIncrease\%$ increase. The boundary therefore presents a feasibility margin issue alongside the angular conditioning requirement.

The reported RMS describes local first-order uncertainty. Raw intersections of noisy bearings can have heavy tails when the measured lines are nearly parallel, including under nominally well-conditioned geometry.
