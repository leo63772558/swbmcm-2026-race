Problem 2(1) chooses two vehicles and unobstructed stations for alert $T_2=(50+45\cos150^\circ,\,50+45\sin150^\circ,\,35)$~m, with each vehicle arriving within 30~s. Target altitude is known. We minimize the local horizontal localization error from two azimuth bearings with independent Gaussian noise of standard deviation $\sigma_\theta=1^\circ=\pi/180$~rad \cite{bishop2010}.

\subsection{Bearing Jacobian and Horizontal Covariance}
Let two vehicles occupy distinct feasible stations $S_1=(x_1,y_1)$ and $S_2=(x_2,y_2)$ in $\mathcal{F}(T_2)$ at horizontal ranges $r_i=\sqrt{(x-x_i)^2+(y-y_i)^2}$ and true bearings $\alpha_i=\operatorname{atan2}(y-y_i,\,x-x_i)$ toward the target ground projection $T_2'=(x,y)$. The noisy bearing measurement vector is $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}(x,y)+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$ with $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma_\theta^2 I_2)$. The bearing derivatives are
\[
\frac{\partial \alpha_i}{\partial x} = -\frac{y - y_i}{r_i^2} = -\frac{\sin\alpha_i}{r_i}, \qquad
\frac{\partial \alpha_i}{\partial y} = \frac{x - x_i}{r_i^2} = \frac{\cos\alpha_i}{r_i},
\]
These rows form the Jacobian $J$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p2-rms}. First-order propagation around the target gives $\Sigma_p = \sigma_\theta^2 (J^\top J)^{-1}$. Because $|\det(J)| = |\sin(\alpha_2-\alpha_1)|/(r_1 r_2) = \sin\gamma/(r_1 r_2)$, where $\gamma=|\operatorname{wrap}_{[-\pi,\pi)}(\alpha_2-\alpha_1)|\in(0,\pi)$ is the crossing angle between the two lines of sight, the covariance trace is
\[
\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_p) = \sigma_\theta^2 \frac{\|J\|_F^2}{(\det J)^2} = \sigma_\theta^2 \frac{r_1^{-2} + r_2^{-2}}{\sin^2\gamma / (r_1^2 r_2^2)} = \frac{\sigma_\theta^2 (r_1^2 + r_2^2)}{\sin^2\gamma},
\]
Its square root gives the local horizontal RMS in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p2-rms}. Shorter ranges reduce the numerator, and an orthogonal crossing maximizes the denominator.

\begin{keybox}[top=4pt,bottom=4pt]
\small\setlength{\abovedisplayskip}{4pt}\setlength{\belowdisplayskip}{4pt}
\begin{proposition}[Attainment of the Local RMS Lower Bound]
\label{prop:p21-lb}
For a feasible non-collinear pair with independent bearing noise $\sigma_\theta>0$, the local horizontal RMS satisfies
\[
\mathrm{RMS}_{\mathrm{local}}(S_1,S_2) = \frac{\sigma_\theta\sqrt{r_1^2+r_2^2}}{|\sin\gamma|} \ge \sqrt{2}\,\sigma_\theta\,r_{\min}(T_2),
\qquad r_{\min}(T_2)=\frac{H_{T2}-h_c}{\tan\beta_{\max}}\approx5.819\text{ m},
\]
with equality if and only if $r_1=r_2=r_{\min}(T_2)$ and the lines of sight intersect orthogonally ($\gamma=90^\circ$).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The elevation constraint gives $r_i\ge r_{\min}(T_2)>0$, so $\sqrt{r_1^2+r_2^2}\ge\sqrt{2}\,r_{\min}(T_2)$. For a non-collinear pair, $0<|\sin\gamma|\le1$, with equality at the upper end only when $\gamma=90^\circ$. The two inequalities give the bound. Take the station offsets $(x_i-x,y_i-y)=(-r_{\min}/\sqrt{2},\,\pm r_{\min}/\sqrt{2})$ from $T_2'$. They have clear LOS and body clearance. The pair attains the bound, and the route check below establishes reachability.
\end{proof}
\end{keybox}

\subsection{Reachability and Degenerate Station Geometry}
We check reachability on a visibility graph with continuously clear edges and corner waypoints. A shortest-path graph search \cite{dijkstra1959,hart1968} yields arrival times of \BTravelFirst{}~s for V3 and \BTravelSecond{}~s for V5 at the symmetric western stations, approximately $(6.914,68.39)$ and $(6.914,76.61)$~m. Table~\ref{tab:p21} lists this $\BCrossing{}^\circ$ pair with baseline \BBaseLength{}~m.

The covariance is isotropic, with $\Sigma_{xx}=\Sigma_{yy}\approx0.01031$~m\textsuperscript{2} and $\Sigma_{xy}=0$. The circular 95\% error contour in Figure~\ref{fig:p21}b corresponds to a local RMS of \BFirstRMS{}~m. Central finite differences agree with this RMS to relative error below $10^{-10}$.

As $\gamma\to0^\circ$, the two bearings point in almost the same direction. As $\gamma\to180^\circ$, opposed bearings approach the same line and give the same rank deficiency, even though the baseline approaches $r_1+r_2$. In both limits $|\det J|\to0$, so the local RMS grows without bound at fixed positive ranges (Figure~\ref{fig:p21}a). Reject collinear pairs and screen near-collinear pairs symmetrically, for example by keeping $\gamma\in[30^\circ,150^\circ]$. The feasible $90^\circ$ pair in \texttt{P2\_precision.csv} already attains Proposition~\ref{prop:p21-lb}'s lower bound, so this screen preserves the nominal optimum.

At $r_i=r_{\min}$ the pitch constraint is active, but the bearing Jacobian remains nonsingular for interior crossing angles. The nominal stations have zero pitch margin, so an inward range perturbation can violate observability even while the nominal localization error stays finite. A LOS boundary needs separate treatment because contact with a building prism blocks observation. Tightening the allowable elevation to $79^\circ$ moves the T2 inner radius to $\BPrecisionTightRange{}$ m. Moving both stations radially outward preserves the orthogonal intersection. Both stations remain visible and reachable within 30 s, giving an attained minimum RMS of $\BPrecisionTightRMS{}$ m, a $\BPrecisionTightIncrease\%$ increase. The boundary therefore presents a feasibility margin issue alongside the angular conditioning requirement.

The reported RMS describes local first-order uncertainty. Raw intersections of noisy bearings can have heavy tails when the measured lines are nearly parallel, including under nominally well-conditioned geometry.

Among the archived nominal P1, P2(1), and P2(2) routes, only the V3 approach to T2 in P2(1) detours around a building corner. With both endpoints fixed, the waypoint route around the northwest corner of $B_3$ is $\BCornerGraphLength{}$ m. The shortest path around that offset corner consists of two tangents joined by a circular arc of radius $1.5$ m and totals $\BCornerArcLength{}$ m. The disk supplies a length lower bound, attained because both tangents and the arc clear the remaining inflated obstacles. The waypoint route adds $\BCornerAbsoluteGap{}$ m ($\BCornerRelativeGap\%$). This comparison applies only to the audited leg with its endpoints fixed. The dispatch certificate applies to the discrete station pool and waypoint graph.
