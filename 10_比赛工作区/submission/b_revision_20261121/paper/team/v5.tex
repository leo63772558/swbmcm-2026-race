To examine the lone counterexample in our 115-case unique resilience suite (and its \BCaseCount{}-ID archived ledger), we analyze the V5 single-vehicle failure at $t_d=5.000$ s (with active communications) to extract a general control-theoretic principle for real-time fleet recourse. As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:v5} and visualized in the aligned dual-policy Gantt timelines of Figure~\ref{fig:v5}, both Keep Plan and unconditional Reschedule achieve full $3/3$ completion ($\eta=1.000$), yet Reschedule delays the latest paired observation start from $\BKeepLast{}$ s to $\BReplanLast{}$ s—an unexpected regression of $+\BVFiveDelay{}$ s that persists identically across all four tested search budgets ($k\in\{2,3,5,8\}$).

Tracing the micro-level vehicle phase records at $t=5.000$ s explains this paradox completely. Under the frozen nominal $k=8$ plan, V3 and V5 initiated paired observation of alert T1 at $3.093$ s—nearly two seconds \emph{before} V5 fails at $5.000$ s. Because T1 has already started valid paired observation prior to the fault and V5 holds no subsequent slot in the nominal schedule (V3 is the sole reused vehicle, assigned $\mathrm{T1}\!\to\!\mathrm{T3}$), Keep Plan loses zero future capacity: V1 and V4 continue uninterrupted slewing to open T2 at $8.890$ s, while V3 finishes T1 at $8.093$ s, resets its gimbal in $0.984$ s, and pivots cleanly in place to open T3 with V2 at $\BKeepLast{}$ s (Figure~\ref{fig:v5}a).

By contrast, triggering unconditional Reschedule at $t=5.000$ s treats all non-locked alerts (T2 and T3) as open for global reassignment and immediately aborts the in-progress gimbal slews of three healthy vehicles that were already approaching lock-on: V1 (mid-slew toward T2 since $4.177$ s), V2 (mid-slew toward T3 since $2.404$ s), and V4 (mid-slew toward T2 since $2.981$ s). Under Assumption 3, interrupting a slew incurs a mandatory zero-return cancellation reset before any new assignment can commence, imposing sunk delays of $0.823$ s on V1, $2.596$ s on V2, and $2.019$ s on V4 (Figure~\ref{fig:v5}b). Simultaneously, the residual dispatcher reassigns V1 from T2 to T3 (forcing an extra $2.667$ s drive) and diverts reused vehicle V3 from $\mathrm{T1}\!\to\!\mathrm{T3}$ to $\mathrm{T1}\!\to\!\mathrm{T2}$ (adding a $0.647$ s drive plus $3.000$ s slew). Although V2 and V4 eventually re-observe their original targets T3 and T2, their cancellation resets push T3 back to $12.895$ s and shift the global bottleneck onto T2 at $\BReplanLast{}$ s ($+\BVFiveDelay{}$ s slower than Keep Plan).

We term this phenomenon the \textbf{Myopic Reassignment Penalty}: in coupled electro-mechanical fleets where aborting an in-progress actuator transition forfeits accumulated physical progress, blind reactive replanning can forfeit more accumulated slew progress than combinatorial re-optimization recovers whenever baseline feasibility ($\eta_{\mathrm{keep}}=1$) is already preserved. The \emph{two-way threshold selector} of Eq.~\eqref{eq:p4-score} and Algorithm~\ref{alg:p4-reschedule} (lines 12--13) accepts a replan if and only if completion strictly increases ($\eta_{\mathrm{replan}}>\eta_{\mathrm{keep}}$) or ties in completion while cutting latest start beyond an improvement threshold $\delta_{\mathrm{hyst}}\ge 0$.

For the zero-threshold selector ($\delta_{\mathrm{hyst}}=0$ s) and positive-threshold sweeps ($\delta_{\mathrm{hyst}}\in\{0.25,0.5,1.0,2.0\}$ s, \texttt{results/P4\_hysteresis\_sweep.csv}), the selector preserves every Replan win (each has $\Delta\eta>0$) and retains Keep for the V5 fault ($+\BVFiveDelay{}$ s avoided; Table~\ref{tab:app-p4}).
