The T1 location is $T_1=(50+40\cos30^\circ,\,50+40\sin30^\circ,\,45)$~m. Its observable region consists of vehicle centers that satisfy body clearance, gimbal elevation, and line of sight (LOS).

\subsection{Rounded Vehicle Clearance and Optical Occlusion}
The vehicle body stays within the $[0,100]^2$ field exactly when its center $p=(x,y)$ lies in the inset $[1.5,98.5]^2$. For a building footprint $B_k=[x_{k,\min},x_{k,\max}]\times[y_{k,\min},y_{k,\max}]$, the Minkowski offset for a disk of radius $r_v=1.5$~m is \cite{lozano1983}
\[
B_k^{\oplus} = B_k \oplus D(0,r_v) = \bigl\{p\in\mathbb{R}^2 : \operatorname{dist}_2(p,B_k) \le r_v\bigr\}.
\]
Its boundary consists of straight wall offsets and quarter-circle arcs. Since the body may touch the building footprint, admissible centers form the closed set
\[
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{free}} = [1.5,\,98.5]^2 \setminus \bigcup_{k=1}^{5} \operatorname{int}\!\bigl(B_k \oplus D(0,r_v)\bigr).
\]
The LOS set $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{LOS}}(T_1)$ requires the segment from $(x,y,h_c)$ to $T_1$ to miss every closed prism $B_k\times[0,H_k]$. We apply a ray--box slab test in floating-point arithmetic, where near-contact comparisons require care \cite{shewchuk1997}. The resulting observable set is
\[
\mathcal{F}(T_1) = \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{free}} \cap \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{LOS}}(T_1) \cap \bigl\{p\in\mathbb{R}^2 : r(p) \ge r_{\min}(\beta_{\max})\bigr\},
\]
where $r(p)=\|p-T_1'\|_2$ is horizontal range from the ground projection $T_1'$. Table~\ref{tab:p1} and Figure~\ref{fig:p1}a show area estimates. The 0.1~m grid with 1,000,000 cells gives \BGridArea{}~m\textsuperscript{2}. A fixed-seed Monte Carlo check using 250,000 samples gives 5613~m\textsuperscript{2}, with 95\% interval $[5594,5633]$~m\textsuperscript{2}.

\subsection{Optimal Inner Arc, Pitch Sensitivity, and Readiness}
Observation quality $Q(r)=1/r$ decreases with range. The elevation angle is $\beta(r)=\arctan((H-h_c)/r)$, so the upper limit $\beta_{\max}=\BMaxPitch{}^\circ$ requires $r\ge r_{\min}=(45-2)/\tan80^\circ\approx\BMinRange{}$~m. The lower elevation bound is inactive because $H>h_c$. The best achievable quality is $Q_{\max}\approx\BQuality{}$~m\textsuperscript{$-1$}. The body-safe part of the inner circle is a single closed arc of length \BArcLength{}~m, with approximate unwrapped endpoints $\BArcStart{}^\circ$ and $\BArcEnd{}^\circ$ (Figure~\ref{fig:p1}b). LOS excludes no point on this body-safe arc. The disk is tangent to $B_2$ at both endpoints, and vehicle clearance alone removes the intervening $\BArcGap{}^\circ$ sector to the west. At larger ranges LOS shadows also shape the observable boundary.

Differentiating $Q_{\max}=\tan\beta_{\max}/(H-h_c)$ gives $\mathrm{d}Q_{\max}/\mathrm{d}\beta_{\max}=\sec^2\beta_{\max}/(H-h_c)$ with the angle in radians (Eq.~\eqref{eq:p1-quality}). Lowering the ceiling from $80^\circ$ to $79^\circ$ increases the minimum range to 8.358~m and lowers peak quality by \BPitchLoss{}\%. At $60^\circ$ the loss is 69.46\% (Figure~\ref{fig:p1}d).

Among the equal-quality stations, the readiness criterion minimizes driving plus gimbal slew. It selects V4 and a station near $(\BStationX{},\,\BStationY{})$~m at azimuth $\BReadyAngle{}^\circ$. Driving takes \BArrivalTime{}~s, and readiness takes \BReadyTime{}~s. The interval bounding the minimum readiness time has width $7.74\times10^{-5}$~s. Minimizing travel alone selects another station on the same arc. It takes \BAltArrival{}~s to arrive and 6.199~s to become ready (Table~\ref{tab:p1}).

Of the \BRouteCount{} candidate vehicle-to-station routes, rounded clearance shortens \BShortenedCount{} paths by up to \BSavingsMax{}~m and restores \BRestoredCount{} endpoints blocked by axis-aligned rectangular buffers (Figure~\ref{fig:p1}c). The 0.45~mm minimum excess clearance is a critical sample from the candidate pool. The separate audit of 882 executed timeline segments found at least 1.122~m excess clearance after accounting for the vehicle radius.
