For the V5 single-vehicle failure at $t_d=5$ s, Keep and unconditional Reschedule both start all three paired observations. Reschedule moves the latest start from \BKeepLast{} s to \BReplanLast{} s, a delay of \BVFiveDelay{} s across all four tested search budgets (Table~\ref{tab:v5}).

V3 and V5 start T1 at $3.093$ s. T1 counts under the first-start rule even though V5 fails before the dwell ends. V5 has no later slot. With Keep, V1 and V4 start T2 at $8.890$ s. V3 finishes its scheduled T1 dwell at $8.093$ s, resets over $0.9844$ s, and observes T3 with V2 from \BKeepLast{} s.

At $5$ s, unconditional Reschedule interrupts V1, V2, and V4 during slew. Their gimbals take $0.8233$, $2.596$, and $2.019$ s, respectively, to return to zero. V1 then drives $2.667$ s from its T2 station to a new T3 station. V3 switches from T3 to T2, with an extra $0.647$ s of travel and a $3.000$ s slew to T2. T3 starts at $12.90$ s and T2 becomes the bottleneck at \BReplanLast{} s.

We call this delay the \emph{myopic reassignment penalty}. The new assignments lose more time to cancellation and setup than they recover. The zero-threshold selector in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p4-score} keeps the cached plan for V5 because completion is tied and reassignment delays the latest start. Across the 115 deterministic scenarios, the selector retains \BHystWins{} wins against Keep with \BHystTies{} ties and \BHystLosses{} losses.
